# Real-world photo references for art direction

Use when the user asks for real-life references, including anatomy, seasons, terrain, materials or construction. Use photographs as physical evidence for the feature they actually demonstrate; they do not establish fictional canon, hidden geometry or creative approval.

## Procedure

1. **Set the filter before searching.** For landscapes, identify season/month, biome/species and the features that matter; late-spring woodland does not establish a March ground condition. For materials, identify the substrate, coating, exterior/interior exposure and the visible cue to demonstrate before choosing search terms.
   - For historic architecture, distinguish the structure's date from the date/status of its cladding and finishes. A medieval frame may wear repaired or reconstructed boards; label modern museum reconstructions and unknown photo dates, and separate a photo observation from an owner's explicit claim about original fabric.
   - Verify substrate and finish independently through an owner, museum, conservation report or survey. A pale wall alone does not distinguish limewash, paint or stain; retain a documented generic finish name without inventing its composition. Apply the identification only to the described surface: plaster infill beside a coated timber, or old peeling paint beside new boards, does not inherit the timber's finish identification.
2. **Find the source that owns the feature, then find usable photographs.** For historical construction, start with museum/owner descriptions, conservation guidance and archaeological reports; seek a joint drawing or section when the claim concerns hidden construction. For searchable licensed photographs, query Wikimedia Commons through its API from `execute_code`: `action=query&generator=search&gsrnamespace=6&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=url|extmetadata&iiurlwidth=800`. Read `LicenseShortName`, `DateTimeOriginal`, `Artist` and `descriptionurl`; an owner's photograph is reference-only unless reuse rights are separately established.
   - Start each query with `filetype:bitmap`. Without it, scanned books and PDFs crowd out the photos.
   - Use short plain phrases: "woodland floor", "oak woodland March", "fallen oak", "root plate", "leaf litter woodland", "dead bracken woodland March". German terms ("Wurzelteller", "Laubwald März") reach a different pool of photographers. Long specific queries return nothing.
   - Geograph (UK) uploads are a productive source: CC BY-SA, with an exact capture date and a descriptive title. Filter them by month after the search.
   - Parse `DateTimeOriginal` as ISO or free text, including month names and “Taken on”/“created” forms; ISO-only filtering silently loses valid photographs. Keep unparsed dates unknown rather than classifying them off-season, and distinguish capture date from upload date.
   - Region names pull unrelated uploads (airports, bases, colleges, rock specimens). Query landscapes by viewpoint (`<peak> from <town>`, `<range> from plains`) and by landform (`cirque`, `treeline`, `talus`), then drop the noise with an exclude regex before building the contact sheet.
   - Send a descriptive User-Agent. Wait about 4 s between API calls and about 30 s after an HTTP 429, retrying once. Back-to-back bursts get throttled for minutes.
3. **Write everything to an absolute scratch path.** `execute_code` runs in the project working directory and may not have `TMPDIR` set, so a relative path drops downloads into the repo. In the bash terminal `TMPDIR` can be the POSIX `/tmp`, which native Windows Python resolves to `<cwd drive>:\tmp`, so pass native programs the full scratch path. Run `git status` afterwards if unsure.
4. **Inspect the smallest useful reference set.** Use thumbnails and a numbered PIL contact sheet when comparing many candidates, then inspect selected individual images large enough to resolve the required feature. A comparable building plus a joint drawing can answer a construction question better than a collection of exterior photographs. For landscapes, discard wrong-season/biome shots and note ingredients and relief scale; for distant landforms, match the game's foreground and viewing distance. Label an off-season image retained only for structure.
5. **Add physical causes when they matter.** For example, windthrow leaves pit-and-mound pairs, and litter and snow collect in pits. Get these from primary or academic sources, so the proposal explains why the features occur and not only what they look like.
6. **Show the evidence that answers the question.** For scene selection, deliver the contact sheet via `MEDIA:` with title, capture month and demonstrated feature. For construction feasibility, lead with the inspected example or joint drawing, original/reconstructed status, and a plain-language account of the assembly; state exactly what differs from the proposed asset. For an appearance gap, show one target-vs-ours panel with the approved target, matched current view and closest useful references. Keep licence, artist and URL; label scratch copies temporary and obtain authorization before saving into another project/research repo. CC BY-SA requires attribution.
7. **Get exact credits in one call.** Search results truncated or wrapped in HTML are not usable credits. Send one `action=query&titles=A|B|...&prop=imageinfo&iiprop=extmetadata` request for all the keepers. HTML-unescape `Artist` and strip its tags before any truncation, because Geograph artists arrive as profile links.
8. **Archive the selected set only when authorized.** Follow the existing subject/topic layout in the designated research library. Copy unchanged selected originals and any already useful contact sheet; do not create another sheet or catalog merely to archive them. Extend the existing index or one concise README with demonstrated feature, limits, source/image URLs, credit, rights, capture date or explicit unknown, and SHA-256. Preserve unrelated index edits and link the production plan to this index rather than duplicating the reference library.

## Verify an authorized archival handoff

1. Inspect the existing artifact/context storage and publication checks before insertion. Reuse that mechanism for original image bytes, provenance and rights; treat real-world analogues as research context, not new fictional canon or creative approval. A local archival request grants neither production-pixel reuse nor external publication.
2. Deduplicate against retained paths and byte hashes. When database-only retrieval is required, retain actual image bytes and subject/context links rather than paths alone. Register the exact additions in the existing inventory/publication expectations without replacing old baseline assertions; follow the [structured publication checks](structured-migration.md#4-verify-the-entire-inventory).
3. Rehearse on a recoverable copy and reapply the same input: IDs and row counts must remain stable. For a context-only addition, verify that supported facts, chronology and approved/proposed decisions remain unchanged while only the intended context/images become retrievable.
4. Read back the installed records, compare embedded bytes with the archived files and hashes, resolve index/plan links, and run the retained project checks. Report the maintained location and archive status separately from design selection or texture clearance.

## Biological anatomy: evidence fit

1. **Define the surface and condition.** Name the relevant species/anatomical analogue, sex where applicable, body region and intended pose/state before searching. Seek whole-body stance context plus regional skin-intact views from complementary angles; a query focused only on one organ can return reproductive or clinical examples rather than the ordinary junction being modeled.
2. **Screen eligibility before selection.** Verify subject and context from the original page, then inspect the exact image pixels. Keep pregnancy/lactation, age, health, pose and capture date explicitly known or unknown. Use dissection for underlying structure and pregnancy, foaling or clinical images only for the relationships they demonstrate; they do not establish neutral exterior proportions. An unspecified reproductive state is not confirmed neutral.
3. **Assign a role per photograph.** Distinguish a surface-target candidate, structural/pose context and an excluded example. A small rear view may show thigh mass without identifying sex or revealing a tail-obscured junction. Crops, shadows and overlapping limbs do not establish hidden attachments or a measured clearance; camera/stance differences can change apparent negative space.
4. **Show the usable comparison.** Place the intact accepted model and original marked concern beside the selected real photographs. Label what tissue/volume must remain and which transition is being questioned; keep a generated design guide separate from anatomical evidence. State view differences instead of implying calibrated projection or transferring another individual's breed, condition or proportions.
5. **Carry qualifications into the handoff.** Add demonstrated region, reference role, biological-state limits and occluded areas to the existing provenance record. Reuse the procedure's source/image URLs, credit, rights and exact-file hashes rather than creating another catalogue. State an unresolved view gap plainly; retain context-only references as context instead of upgrading them to complete surface authority.

## Historical construction: evidence fit

Define the proposed assembly first: load-bearing frame versus infill/cladding, interior versus exterior, support and joint arrangement, period/region, and relevant weather exposure. Then classify each reference:

| Evidence | What it can establish | Qualification to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Excavated timbers or joints | Observed profiles, fixings and materials | Original placement or the complete building may remain uncertain |
| Standing historic building | A surviving construction method | Separate original fabric, repairs, later cladding and maintenance |
| Archaeologically based reconstruction | A full-scale working interpretation | Identify conjectural parts, modern interventions and the reconstruction date |
| Different building type or exposure | A transferable joint or structural principle | It does not prove the whole proposed domestic/exterior assembly |
| Exterior photograph | Visible orientation, profile and finish | It cannot establish concealed joints or the age of individual boards |

Conclude with the demonstrated mechanism, the proposed adaptation and the remaining unsupported match. Keep these levels separate even when combining several sources; useful analogues do not become an excavated or surviving example of the exact proposed house.
