# PCG ground generation: relief, rocks, snow, deadwood and floor clutter

Use when explicit Generate must shape terrain (rises, gullies, pits, mounds), crown rises with rock outcrops, place residual snow, or scatter forest-floor clutter that scales to new woodland without hand placement.

## Split relief by terrain resolution

1. Read the Landscape quad spacing first. A grid of N metres cannot show features narrower than about 2N: pits and hummocks on a coarse regional grid vanish between samples. Write broad relief (knolls, swales, pits ≥ ~2 grid cells) into the Landscape; deliver smaller relief (root plates, hummocks, rock shelves) as collision meshes placed by Generate.
2. Make relief a pure function of world XY plus a seed (Perlin octaves for knolls/swales, deterministic per-cell site selection for windthrow pairs). New tiles then join old ones seamlessly, and repeat Generate is exact.
3. Write into a dedicated, named edit layer, created once and reordered beneath the spline layer: splines alpha-blend over lower layers, so roadbeds keep their authored grade and hand sculpting on other layers is never overwritten. Read the layer back first and skip the write when the heights already match, so repeat Generate is a no-op.
4. Mask relief to zero with smooth fades, never hard cut-offs: around road edges (fade only across the road's own shoulder, a few metres; ground character must not depend on where a road runs, see "Terrain first, road second" below), around clearing boxes (distance outside the box, not inside/outside), and across the detail tile's outer transition collar so it still meets coarse terrain exactly. A hard mask edge leaves a step as tall as the relief.
5. Place deadfall from the same site list as the pits: root plate on the mound facing the pit, trunk from the mound along the fall direction. Check the trunk line against trees and the mask; centre the mesh by its bounds so pivot conventions do not matter.
6. For relief that reads as hilly from player height, layer at least three noise scales (~90 m hills, ~35 m rises, ~12 m swales, weights summing to 1 so the amplitude stays a hard cap) and target about ±3 m of measured change; ±1 m knolls average ~10 cm and still look flat. Dry gullies: `-Depth * Channel²` where `Channel = 1 - smoothstep(0, 0.07, |noise(P/60 m)|)` traces the noise zero-line, multiplied by a second noise's smoothstep to break it into segments. Do not answer stronger relief with a longer road fade: a ~24 m fade flattened the whole band roadside cameras look across (3° median slope vs ~7° beyond) and made the forest read flat. Fit the road to the ground instead. Add every new strength control with a default of 0/off so other maps' graphs stay unchanged, and report the measured change rather than the configured cap. Multi-octave Perlin rarely reaches its cap: a two-octave ~6 m/~4 m lump term measured only ~40-45% of its nominal height (setting 100 cm gave -46..+39 cm, RMS 17 cm). Measure it before judging visuals: dump landscape-only heights on a 1 m grid over a forest patch well beyond the road fade, at the 0 setting and the trial, using `SystemLibrary.line_trace_multi` and keeping the first hit whose actor is a `LandscapeProxy`. A multi-trace still stops at the first blocking hit, so samples under rocks return nothing (about 20% here): report the sample count, compare only keys present in both dumps, and do not use it for single-point cross-sections. Then report min/max/RMS of the difference.
   - Set expectations before proposing terrain relief at the grid limit: bumps of ~2-3 grid cells read only as gentle lumpiness at player height. The floor's vertical character in the 0.3-3 m band (rubble, root humps, litter mounds, rock shelves) comes from collision meshes, not the heightfield. Relief also moves slope-following placements (rock sites, clusters), so judge an A/B from fixed review views plus the height dump, not from low cameras that new rocks may block.
   - Eye-level character comes from how many landforms a view crosses, not their height. Sparse broken ridges and one-sided banks (crests on a ~50 m noise contour, banks on ~70 m) read clearly on a hillshade but barely changed eye-level views, and raising their height only pooled snow in the new hollows (largest patch 47 → 124 m²). Their banks were already near the grid's steepness limit (~2.5 m over 4 m), so "steeper" is not the lever either: check the measured steepest step against the grid spacing before proposing sharper transitions. What worked: halve every noise scale (twice as many ridges and banks) and widen the bank's smoothstep band by the same factor, so each bank keeps its ~3 m run instead of becoming a cliff. At 250 cm that gave typical slope 6-8° → 9-11°, steepest 10% ~14° → 22-26°, max ~42°, largest snow patch 68 m². Before judging a relief trial from generic review views, diff the before/after height grids, pick the 3-4 steepest added steps, and aim cameras straight at them; if those don't read either, the feature is too sparse or too broad, not mis-framed. Recheck the snow metrics after every relief change.
   - Judge relief primarily from a box-wide hillshade: sample landscape-only heights on a 4 m grid over the whole review box with downward traces, fill gaps under rocks from neighbours, and render at 3× vertical exaggeration, then follow up with eye-level views. Render each map from two opposite light azimuths before calling a streak pattern an artefact: streaks running perpendicular to the light flip with it and are shading, not terrain; confirm with the mean height step along N-S, E-W and both diagonals, which stays within ~1.4× for unbiased noise.
7. Terrain first, road second. The user's standing direction: forest ground is never flat; it has ridges, banks and larger ups and downs, and the road is a dirt cart track that follows that ground rather than shaping it. A spline must be movable anywhere without the surroundings depending on it. When the road's influence keeps fighting forest development, removing it and rebuilding it later is an accepted workflow:
   - Inventory first: spline segments per Landscape (`LandscapeService.get_spline_info`), road-detail actors (embedded stones, bank meshes) and edge joins. Locate pieces by `SystemLibrary.get_component_bounds` on each `SplineMeshComponent`; `get_spline_info` positions are not world coordinates, even through the spline component transform.
   - Export `get_spline_info` to JSON, and note the last commit with the road as the recovery point.
   - Delete only the route inside the work tile: `LandscapeService.delete_all_splines(landscape)` then `rebuild_splines(landscape)`, and read back 0 points, segments and spline meshes. Remove road-detail instances inside the tile with `ism.modify()` plus `remove_instances(indices)`. Keep regional pieces at the tile edges as reconnection joins.
   - `WOTForest` reads roads from every Landscape's splines, so the same Generate drops both the relief fade and the tree/rock clearance there. Regenerate, then re-measure slope against the old centreline.
   - Review cameras defined on the road now stand inside trees and rocks: pick new forest views before judging the ground.
   - Rebuilding the road later changes road geometry, so update the spec's road-geometry and road-clearance gates when the road comes out.
8. Rock outcrops: gate cells by the normalized rise value (`Broad(P,seed,1)`) so rocks crown high ground, then place one anchor boulder plus 3-9 members spreading outward and shrinking toward scree; skip members near trees or outside the mask, and scale `GroundOffset` with each member's size. Scanned 4 m boulders need scale ~0.45.

## Residual snow from a Generate field

- Keep one scalar cover field on a 1 m grid as the single source: the physical sheet is its zero iso-line, and materials read the same field so ground snow, sheet and plant bases agree.
- Bias the field toward hollows (ground below a summed-area-table box mean of ~8 m, clamped to ±1 m) and shade (uphill gradient · direction toward the level's `DirectionalLight`). Store road/exclusion/tile-edge limits separately and apply `min(field + bias, limit)`, so bias can never push snow onto a road. Verify with snow fraction by landform (hollow / flat / rise from the height dump); hollows should clearly exceed rises while total coverage stays on target.
- Export the field from C++ in the editor to a G8 `UTexture2D` (`Source.Init` between `PreEditChange(nullptr)` and `PostEditChange()`, grayscale, non-sRGB, clamp), and compare with `Source.GetMipData` first so an unchanged field leaves the package clean. Map texel X,Y to the grid origin + 1 m·(X,Y) and sample with `SampleLevel 0`.
- Make ground snow extend slightly beyond the sheet (cover `smoothstep(-0.05, 0.01, field + noise)`) so the sheet's edge lands on snow, add a slushy grey rim inside and a darker wet-litter ring outside, and break the edge with noise down to ~12 cm. Frost the lowest ~14 cm of undergrowth (`LocalPosition.z`) where cover is set, for partial burial.
- A smooth sheet on a 1 m grid reads flat however good the material. Give snow 3D character with seeded Nanite clump meshes (polar heightfield: lobed outline, gaussian lumps, `(1-s^3.5)^0.45` shoulder, last ring sunk ~4 cm) placed by the snow node from the same field: dense in the edge band (field -0.12..0), thinner on the sheet, Z from an `FDynamicMeshAABBTree3` ray cast onto the sheet. Spawner without collision: set the spawner's template descriptor collision to off explicitly and read it back. Saving the clump meshes with `collision=False` only omits simple shapes; the spawner kept query+physics, so instances likely (not tested in PIE) got physics bodies while the player capsule still passed through. Set both `body_instance.collision_profile_name='NoCollision'` and `collision_enabled=NO_COLLISION` on the descriptor, write the body instance and descriptor back, Generate, then read `get_collision_enabled()` on the generated ISMs. ~166k clumps cost ~3.7 ms in the Editor flythrough.
- Leaf litter belongs on the strip the melting edge has just uncovered, not in hollows: snow already fills the hollows, so a hollow-biased litter rule mostly moves litter under snow (it raised drifts in hollows 32% → 49% while drifts off snow fell 589 → 346). Place edge litter from the snow node itself with the same field: chance `smoothstep(-0.2,-0.1,F)·(1-smoothstep(-0.04,0,F))` just outside the sheet. Lay the long side across the field gradient (which points into the snow), and keep it default-off (spacing 0). Measured: median 2 m from snow, max 7 m. For any terrain-form placement rule, report the share of litter on snow next to the rule's own metric. Read it offline from the exported G8 field: texel = `Area.Min` + 1 m·(x, y), snow where byte > 127.
- PCG spawner `mesh_selector_parameters` is read-only from Python: edit the selector object in place (`sel.set_editor_property('template_descriptor', d)`), then read back.
- Check regional evenness, not just the tile total: bin the exported field into ~50 m blocks and correlate block coverage with each input (shelter, noise). A tree-shelter term follows regional stand density and makes whole halves heavy or bare; remove its ~50 m regional mean (summed-area table) and add back the tile mean, keeping local under-crown contrast. Export the G8 field with `AssetExportTask` to PNG (read-only) for this analysis.
- Components a custom node creates on the PCG actor (for example a `UDynamicMeshComponent` snow sheet) must carry `PCGHelpers::DefaultPCGTag` as well as any project tag. `GetGridBounds`/`GetActorBounds` skip only components with that tag, so an untagged sheet inflates the volume bounds every node samples from, and shrinking the volume appears to do nothing. Read a volume's real extent from its actor location and scale (`scale * 100` half-extent), not from `get_actor_bounds`, which includes generated content.
- To iterate on a smaller area, clip object placement to the PCG volume behind a default-off setting. Use one test after all candidates are assembled, outside any optional branch (a filter inside `if (ReliefLand)` skipped every authored-candidate node), and mark snow samples outside the volume invalid. Keep relief written over the whole tile, so the terrain stays fixed and expanding the volume back restores the same forest. Record the volume's original transform before shrinking it. Verify by counting instances outside the box per mesh, including skinned instances (read serialized `instance_data`; `get_instance_id` is not materialized in 5.8); native spline-sampler branches keep points whose bounds overlap the volume, so they leak tens of metres. `cleanup(True)` completes over later ticks: confirm the actor has no generated components in a separate call before Generate, or stale components still set the grid bounds.
- Uniform density: per-50 m-band instance counts for every element are the check. Growth areas and the PCG volume must cover the generated tile with their fringe outside it; a treeline crossing the tile cascades into every tree-proximity rule. Features that need open ground in a full stand (windthrow, outcrops) should clear the trees they overlap rather than avoid them. Fix snow share with a target-coverage quantile shift, not a hand-tuned offset, so it stays put when tree density changes.
- More snow on request: raise `WOTSpringSnow.TargetCoverage` only, leaving `ShadeBias`/`HollowBias`/`Breakup`, so the extra cover stays in shade and hollows. Coverage is quantile-normalized, so the result tracks the target; say so instead of claiming a measured value. If the spec caps coverage, record the change there as the Game Director's decision. More cover makes the snow material dominant: check a close floor view for smooth, hard-edged slabs and flag it as the next fix rather than tuning coverage further.
- Snow surface: real snow textures at 2-3 m and ~0.7 m tiling (a repurposed ice material tiled hundreds of times reads as flat white), roughness ~0.55-0.85, sparse view-dependent glints faded beyond ~15 m to avoid shimmer.

## Order generation after the terrain write

- Sibling PCG nodes fed by one sampler run in parallel. Wire the terrain-writing node's output into each grounding node's native `Execution Dependency` pin (exists on almost every node; label `Execution Dependency`), including road-verge projection branches.
- In the writer, prepare candidates once in a custom context, then return `false` from `ExecuteInternal` until `ALandscape::IsUpToDate()` so grounding traces read the merged heights and collision.
- C++ that writes heights through `FHeightmapAccessor` needs the `Foliage` module in Build.cs (the accessor references `AInstancedFoliageActor`); compile succeeds and only the link fails without it.

## Floor clutter that reads at player height

- Judge clutter from a standing-height view, not instance counts. Tree-crown twig parts (~1 cm thick) are sub-pixel beyond a couple of metres and lie as upright wire fans; scanned debris packs can be 3-30 cm chips. Readable deadwood is 2-8 cm thick, 0.5-2.5 m long.
- When owned meshes miss that role, generate many seeded variants rather than a few hand-made meshes: a handful of identical sticks reads as copy-pasted however widely scattered. Use the seeded generator and material recipe in `unreal-asset-sourcing` `references/procedural-stand-ins.md` for deadwood (mostly straight, tapered, splintered); the user rejected generated grass tufts, bare shrubs, brambles and leaf drifts, so source those roles as scans first. Nanite on, collision off.
- Scatter on a world grid with per-cell deterministic jitter, then thin by acceptance probability: higher under crowns (near trees) and modulated by low-frequency noise, so clutter clusters instead of forming an even grid. Lower the spacing to compensate for the rejection rate. Tilt each item to the ground over its own half-length and sink long pieces to the lowest sampled support so ends do not float over swales.
- Floor spawner: no collision and a modest cull distance. Deadfall spawner: collision. Mesh collision and spawner collision are separate layers: record which one a "no collision" claim describes, and read back the spawner descriptor before writing it into evidence.
- Tree-distance rules alone let floor items pass through each other and through tufts. Give palette entries a default-off footprint and reject overlaps in placement order on a coarse grid (each footprint listed in every cell its bounds touch):
  - Shape: a 2D capsule along the mesh's longer XY bounds axis spanning its full length. Scale only the radius (the shorter half-extent). Forks and stubs make a stick's bounds far wider than its wood, so a uniformly scaled capsule over-rejects and silently thins the floor. Sticks ~0.3, fallen logs with branch stubs ~0.3, tuft core ~0.3, shrubs ~0.25, moss ~0.5, chips 1.0, root plates ~0.8, rocks ~0.9. Size a footprint for a branchy mesh from the wood, not its bounds: on a placed collision instance, cast downward complex traces (`SystemLibrary.line_trace_single`, hit component at `to_tuple()[10]`) across it at ~8 fractions along its length. Take the median hit half-width over the shorter bounds half-extent, rounded up slightly so items touch the bark instead of entering it. Scanned fallen trunks measured ~0.24–0.29.
  - Seed the grid with the larger pieces placed first (root plates, fallen logs, outcrop rocks), so floor items avoid them. Rock-on-rock overlap inside an outcrop is intended scree.
  - Changing a large obstacle's footprint is a floor-density change: the freed strip beside it fills with new items. Report per-category count deltas and the overlap pairs that involve that obstacle. When you fix the checker between states, compare both states on the category set they share.
  - Measure overlap pairs per category pair before and after with a checker that rebuilds footprints from the generated instances and mesh bounds. It shows the rule works, not that nothing visibly clips, so pair it with a capture. Residual pairs come from ground tilt and from candidates the final accept pass drops after they already blocked neighbours.
  - Report per-mesh count drops: rejection is a density change. Offer spacing compensation rather than applying it unasked. Candidate count scales with 1/spacing², so a ~10% spacing cut restores ~20% of items.
- Count per category inside the iteration box (ISM instances by mesh-name substring, filtered to the box XY) before and after every density change. Pick the control by the intent:
  - **More of it where it already grows** (a relative increase such as "+30%"): scale spacing by `1/sqrt(1+x)`, for example 95 → 83 cm for +30%. This is predictable, measured +29%, and keeps the patch layout.
  - **Wider patches, reaching new ground:** lower the patch-noise threshold. It is steeply non-linear (lowering a grass-mat threshold by 0.10 doubled the mats), so step it by ~0.05 and report the measured multiplier.
  - **Richer layer (more and bigger of an adopted scan):** duplicate its existing entries with a larger uniform `transform` scale (dead shrubs 1.4× → 2.0×, 63–92 cm → 90–130 cm) and `NearObstacle` 0, keeping the original clustered entries. This adds size variety and spread without new assets (box 2,867 → 4,539). Clone entries with `import_text(export_text())` so the per-entry `Material` override carries over, and read it back.
  - **Near-full grass cover** (`GrassThreshold` → ~0.1): see the full-grass-cover rule below before proposing it. Measure cover as the share of a 25 cm raster inside each mat's rotated bounds ellipse (57% → 81% for that threshold change), and diagnose a remaining bare patch by its nearest trees and road splines before changing anything.
- Palette entries are picked uniformly per accepted cell, so adding M entries to an N-entry palette thins every existing entry to N/(N+M). To add new items on top, scale `FloorSpacing` by `sqrt(N/(N+M))` (53 → 65 entries: 100 → 90 cm), then confirm the existing per-category counts held. The same rule doubles one class: duplicate its K entries and scale by `sqrt(N/(N+K))` (64 → 94 entries: 60 → 50 cm doubled grass and dead shrubs while deadwood, stones and cones held within ~10%).
  - Entries with a location filter (`NearObstacle`, `UnderTree`) are worse: every cell that rolls them where the filter fails is lost, so 6 filtered entries in 55 thinned stones and deadwood 10–12% while placing few of their own. Re-roll once in the scatter when the pick's filter fails at the point (a cheap point-level pre-check before the exact footprint test), so only those cells change and the rest of the stream stays deterministic. Weight the filtered role by duplicating its entries, not by loosening the filter.
  - Large clumps (brambles ~3 m) then still displace litter through their footprints (10–17% fewer litter/stones/shrubs for ~1,100 clumps in a 200 m box). Report it as the trade, with the knee-to-chest layer count (shrubs + brambles) beside it.
  - Rare entries (juvenile trees, tall shrubs): give the entry a per-entry `Chance` (default 1) and fold it into the same re-roll helper as the location filter, drawing from the stream only when `Chance < 1` so default entries keep the digest. A missed chance re-rolls instead of leaving the cell empty, so rarity does not thin other litter. Unit-test: plain entry kept without a draw, filter miss re-rolls, Chance 0.25 keeps ~25% over a few thousand rolls.
  - Juvenile trees as floor entries: a skeletal sapling can go through the floor scatter by setting its `OutputPin` to an existing skinned-tree pin (`Pine4`), so it uses that pin's skinned spawner. Floor footprint bounds must also accept `USkeletalMesh` (`GetBounds().GetBox()`), and the node must not write the footprint attribute on skinned-pin points (`@Last` rule below). 4 sapling entries at Chance 0.2 in a ~50-entry palette gave ~575 saplings per Quarry037 map beside ~350 mature pines.
- Per-entry material overrides need the spawner to read them: on the floor spawner's `PCGMeshSelectorByAttribute`, set `use_attribute_material_overrides=True` and `material_override_attributes=[Name('Material')]` (edit the selector object in place, read back). Without that, the entry's `Material` attribute is ignored and instances keep the vendor mesh material; check the generated ISM's `get_material(0)` after Generate.
- Roles without a footprint (grass mats) still cover footprinted items: small litter placed in a heavily grassed area is mostly hidden. Fix it with the `AvoidGrass` placement rule below rather than a mat footprint. Before judging a small item, count how many instances lie clear of mat and tuft centres near the comparison camera, and frame the close-up on one that is clear.

### Placement rules for litter (per palette entry, default off)

Use when litter should follow context: drifts banked against obstacles, species litter under its species, nothing buried under grass, litter at the snow's melting edge (see the residual-snow section).

1. Add each rule as a default-off `FWOTForestCandidate` field, so other graphs and entries are untouched:
   - `bAvoidGrass`: skip a point where the grass-mat patch chance exceeds ~0.25. Factor the mat chance out of the grass loop into one lambda that both use, rather than giving mats footprints, so the two cannot drift apart.
   - `UnderTree` (asset-path substring such as `Pine`): keep the point only within one crown-drip radius (~6 m) of a tree candidate whose asset path matches. Cache the trees per tag on a coarse grid, and build the cache only from trees that survive: candidates are read before windthrow/outcrop clearing and the volume clip remove some. Factor the clearing pass's cleared-tree test into one lambda used by both, and compute the volume bounds once, before the floor, for both the rule and the final clip, so the three cannot drift apart.
   - `BankChance`: before the floor scatter, for each obstacle already in the footprint grid (root plates, fallen trunks, outcrop rocks; not floor items), try twice to lay an item lengthwise against its side. Turn it a quarter if its footprint is long on local Y, offset the pivot to the footprint centre, add a 0.5 cm gap so float noise doesn't count as contact, and step outward 5 cm at a time up to ~60 cm until it clears. Add it to the grid so the scatter fills around it. Apply the same area, tree-distance and grass checks the scatter uses.
   - Unit-test the banking helper: the result clears the obstacle, touches its side, lies parallel, a Y-long mesh gets turned, and it refuses when boxed in.
   - `NearObstacle` (cm): keep the entry only where its footprint comes within that reach of an obstacle in the pre-floor grid (rocks, logs, root plates), for bramble and scrub that gathers at outcrops and deadwood. Keep a separate anchor grid holding only those obstacles, since the ground grid fills with litter. Needs `FootprintScale`; 150 cm gave ~1,100 clumps per 200 m box with two entries per mesh, 80 cm far fewer. Pair it with the re-roll above.
2. Build, then Generate with every new field still off and confirm the iteration-box digest equals the pre-change digest. That proves the rules are opt-in before any tuning.
3. A/B each rule's own target metric, rules off then on, in the same box with a checker. Totals mislead: avoiding grass cut drift count by ~65% while visible drifts barely changed, because the removed ones were buried. Report hidden vs visible, banked vs by-chance contact, and near-species share.
4. Build the checker's categories from what the C++ consults at that stage, not from every footprinted mesh. Bark chips and sticks are footprinted but are not banking obstacles, and counting them inflated the obstacle set ~13× and hid the effect. Match categories by the asset names the palettes actually use: Megascans meshes keep scan IDs (the root plate is `whnpaed_tier_2`, not `RootPlate`), so a role-name match silently counts none. List the actor's ISM mesh names once, and assert that every reported category is non-zero. Pines are skinned instances: read their serialized `instance_data`, and take each `Transform3f`'s `translation` via `get_editor_property` (it has no `.translation` attribute).
5. Measure banking tightness as the capsule gap to each nearest obstacle, splitting banked items from scatter items. An item whose long axis is within ~5° of the obstacle axis came from the banking pass. When banked items sit stepped out, slide each back to touching and list what it would then overlap; that names the blocker. Size a branchy trunk's `FootprintScale` from downward complex traces across placed instances (median wood half-width ÷ bounds half-extent; Quarry037 fallen logs gave ~0.3), not from the bounds. Stepped-out drifts beside a trunk are mostly blocked by its root plate, which is correct. Residual cases with no blocker came from candidates that the final accept pass dropped.
6. Capture banked litter in a snow-free area from above at ~45–50°: snow clumps bury drifts, and a low oblique hides whether the item hugs the obstacle. The snow sheet has no collision, so traces cannot find snow-free sites; frame a few candidate sites and look at them. Leaf drifts barely separate from a leaf-litter ground in direct light, even top-down. When the capture cannot show the change, label it as context and make the measured gaps the evidence.
7. For a matched before/after, set the palette value back, then Generate and capture at the same camera. Re-apply the change and Generate again. The reverted digest must reproduce the committed baseline exactly, and the re-applied one must repeat. That one cycle gives both the comparison and the determinism check.
8. When a species rule keeps a residue of items far from their species, trace each one before changing code. Measure its distance to the nearest surviving tree of that species, to the box edge, and to the nearest clearing obstacle. Items near the edge or near a clearing obstacle point to trees that a later pass removes. Report that cause as likely (not tested) until you apply the surviving-tree filter above and re-measure. The fix is confirmed when the residue reaches 0, the item count falls by exactly the residue, and every other category holds apart from ±1 fill in the freed cells.
9. A node that places litter without seeing the forest's floor output puts it under grass mats and through sticks: without the forest output, 365 of 518 edge drifts landed within 1 m of a mat. Fix it this way:
   - Add an input pin (for example `Ground`) and wire every `WOTForest` node's `Floor` and `Deadfall` outputs into it.
   - Build a coarse grid of (XY, shorter half side × scale) from each point's `Mesh` attribute and bounds.
   - Reject a candidate whose own shorter half plus the item's reaches it. Add each accepted item to the grid so new items don't overlap each other.
   - Keep the grid cell at least the largest summed radius (~2 m), or a 3×3 lookup misses blockers.
   - Expect a density collapse: mats cover about two-thirds of bare ground everywhere, so 400 cm spacing kept 24 drifts. Lower the candidate spacing (150 cm gave 189) rather than relaxing the footprint.
   - With the feature off, the wired pin must still reproduce the baseline digest.
   - A shorter-half circle lets an elongated item lie along a stick or another drift (drift over stick 1 → 42 here). Use the forest's capsules instead (`WOTFootprint.h`) for both the ground items and the new item, with each ground item's own `FootprintScale` passed as a point attribute: full-width capsules from bounds cut edge drifts 198 → 41, forest widths gave 304 with zero overlaps.
   - Pass extra attributes only on points that need them: PCG skinned-mesh spawners default to `@Last`, and a second attribute on tree points silently dropped all 379 Pines (a `LogPCG: Error` in the Generate log, nothing in the counts unless you check skinned instances).
- Generated meshes: never set asset properties (`support_ray_tracing`, etc.) on `ModelingService`-saved meshes after the fact. The rebuild inflates their bounds beyond the geometry, footprints widen, and the next Generate moves floor items. Compare `get_bounding_box()` with the source mesh bounds (GeometryScript) when a floor reshuffles unexpectedly.
- A new builder in a shared generator script (`forest_undergrowth.py`) must not call the shared material builder (`ensure_material()`): it re-saves a material every existing generated mesh uses. Assert the material exists and reuse it.
- Underbrush must read at standing height, not just exist: thin dark canes on a dark floor vanish beyond ~4 m. Trial a new shrub type with two transient `StaticMeshActor`s placed 3.5–4.5 m in front of a fixed camera (tagged, destroyed before saving), compare against the old type in the same frame, and check it close up too: added leaf cards and brighter tints made generated brambles readable at 4 m but read as paper from 1 m, and the user rejected them. Prefer a scanned asset (`unreal-asset-sourcing`).
- To remove a mesh role, search every node's settings for the asset path, not only `FloorPalette`: the snow node's `EdgeLitterMeshes` also placed the drifts. Iterate `dir(settings)`, read each property and match the path in list entries' `export_text()`; empty each list (an empty `EdgeLitterMeshes` disables that loop), Generate, and assert 0 instances of the mesh on every ISM in the whole map, not just the box. Keep the assets and record the removal in the spec where it overrode an accepted line.
- When the user selects a generated item in the viewport, the selection reports the whole PCG actor. Identify the mesh by tracing the viewport camera's centre ray (`get_level_viewport_camera_info`) to the first `LandscapeProxy` hit and listing ISM instances within ~2.5 m of it, nearest first; hide that mesh's components (`set_visibility(False)`) and capture to confirm before acting.
- Taking floor items out of ray tracing: first time it and capture it per item class in one PIE session. Toggle `visible_in_ray_tracing` on each class's components as the control and bracket off/on/off captures; whole-floor crops hide a class-level change. Quarry037: tufts were ~0.19 of a ~0.23 ms saving and lit ~5% flatter, while sticks, drifts and cones saved ~0.04 ms. Only then split a spawner (own `OutputPin` plus a template descriptor with ray tracing off; never the mesh flag, see above).
- Procedural stand-ins for organic forms (moss hummocks, cushions, leaf-drift mounds, bramble tangles) rarely reach the realism bar next to scanned ground: a seeded lobed reshape (`forest_undergrowth.moss_hummock`) still read as an unnatural blob with a hard material rim, and generated drifts and brambles were removed as poor quality in favour of Fab assets. When the user calls such a prop unnatural, offer removal or a real scanned/owned asset alongside one reshape, and stop after one rejected pass rather than moving on to material polish. If you do reshape: palette entries have no weight, so keep the slot count fixed (adding variants multiplies the category), and keep scanned ground textures at ~1 m tiling (2 m on a 2-4 m cushion read as a pebbled rock slab).
- Make each new per-map cut-off or strength a node setting whose default reproduces the old behaviour, and set the new value only on the target graph: other graphs keep their digests, the existing tests stay valid, and the user can retune with a Generate instead of a rebuild.
- Full grass cover (`GrassThreshold` ~0.1) collides with every grass-avoiding rule: `bAvoidGrass` drifts and cones drop to 0 and the snow node's edge drifts collapse against mats. Count them first and ask whether litter should vanish or lie on the grass; for "on the grass", turn `bAvoidGrass` off. Expect litter counts to jump (Quarry037: drifts ×3.4, cones ×3.4, edge drifts ×2.6) because grass had been excluding most of the floor; report it and offer trimming rather than applying it.
  - Grass and snow mutually exclusive (bushes may still stand out of snow): route the mats, not the whole floor, through the snow node. Grass palette entries use `OutputPin` `Grass`; `WOTForest.Grass` → `WOTSpringSnow.Grass` (drops points whose bilinear field is ≥ `GrassSnowEdge`) → the floor spawner's `In`. Mats then never reach the snow node's `Ground`, so edge litter lies over grass with no ignore list. A placement that must avoid snow belongs in the node that owns the field: the forest node runs before the field exists.
  - Run that filter before the sheet build: the connected branch has early `return true` paths (empty sheet, invalid topology), and a filter placed after them silently drops all grass whenever the sheet is rejected.
  - The join must read seamless: grass runs right up to the snow and slightly under it, with no bare strip. Use `GrassSnowEdge` 0 (the sheet edge). The −0.03 default, the clumps' on-sheet cut, is in field units, not distance: it drops snow-free grass up to about one field cell (`Spacing`, 5 m on Quarry037) from the sheet and leaves a visible dirt band. Positive values let mats creep further under.
  - Test a field filter with: no kept point under the sheet, a non-empty sheet (else that check passes vacuously), and the parameter's direction (edge 0 keeps more than −0.03). Do not assert a guessed distance bound for the edge band: log the measured worst distance with `AddInfo` first, since the band width follows the field grid and bilinear-vs-triangle interpolation.
  - Under-crown cover is `WOTForest.GrassUnderCrown` (default 0.55 = shade thinning; 1.0 fills up to the trunk clearance). "Almost all the floor" overrides the shade default; confirm it if the user earlier chose thinning. The bare ring at each trunk is `WOTForest.GrassTrunkClearance` (mat-centre distance from tree centre, default 70 cm; 30 cm lets grass meet the trunk). Measure it as grass share of snow-free ground 40–100 cm from tree centres.
  - Report cover as grass share of snow-free ground, grass share of the snow-free band within 2 m of snow (the seam metric; ~96% at edge 0), and grass share of the snow area (mat edges tuck under the sheet: ~5% at −0.03, ~13% at 0), alongside snow share. Compute the band with a separable running-window dilation of the snow raster. Snow mask: 1 m downward `line_trace_component(start, end, True, False, False)` on the sheet's `DynamicMeshComponent`s; it returns `None` on a miss.
- Before a batch measurement script calls an unfamiliar Python binding (GeometryScript queries, component traces), probe it once on one sample and print the return type: out-parameter order and miss values (`None` vs a tuple) differ per binding, and a wrong guess burns the two-attempt limit on the full batch.
- To change `WOTForest` tree density, change growth density (`GrowthSpacing`), but tune it by measuring, not with 1/√k. The count follows roughly spacing^−1.4: on Quarry037, +25% needed 400 → 340 cm, not 358, partly because added windthrow clears more trees. To change fallen trees, scale `WindthrowChance`. It is stable per cell, so existing sites stay. Each added site brings a trunk, root plate and pit/mound, and rewrites the relief layer plus `T_QR*_SnowField` (tree shelter feeds the snow field). Any Generate that changes the floor or snow can re-dirty `Snow/T_QR*_SnowField`: include it in the save allowlist as an expected generated output, and still stop on any other unexpected package.
- Tree density from `GrowthSpacing` saturates: Quarry037 followed roughly spacing^−1.4 down to 340 cm but only spacing^−0.9 below that (+25% needed 340 → 255). `WindthrowChance` caps at 1.0, so beyond that, raise fallen-tree counts with a smaller `WindthrowCellSize`. That re-rolls every site, which moves existing fallen trees.
- Do not trust `WOTForest`'s "rewrote … relief" log after any PIE cycle in the session; it stops tracking changes. To verify relief, record landscape-only line probes in a clean session, compare against them, and check persistence with a cold Editor start.
- Live Coding is a test loop only for PCG nodes: the map's Generate kept running the pre-patch element. Judge Generate output after a real build.
- When a second feature joins an existing generation block, give each loop its own guard (`if (bClumps) for …`, `if (bLitter) for …`) instead of one shared `if (bA || bB)` with the switch in the loop condition: the disabled loop's initializer still computes `FloorToInt(Area.Min/Spacing)` with spacing 0. That is undefined behaviour which happens to yield the right output, so the off-digest check cannot catch it; only review or a sanitizer does.
- Trial a colour change on generated meshes as variants of the existing build: same seeds, so the geometry is unchanged. Scale the leaf/needle vertex colours past the mound's vertex range in a throwaway call. Capture each variant from one standing-height shaded camera, waiting ~25 s after each rebuild, and crop them into one labelled A/B/C panel.
  - Bake only the chosen multiplier into the script's colour constants. `srgb()` is a linear multiply with gamma 1, so scaling the inputs equals scaling the stored colours.
  - Restore the trial assets from Git with the Editor closed, so it doesn't reload them.
  - The iteration-box digest hashes mesh paths and transforms, so it stays identical through a colour-only rebuild (assert that, since it proves geometry held); the panel is the colour evidence.
- When the amount of a vegetation role grows, recheck its colour against the direction doc at the comparison view. A small hue mismatch, such as green mats against an olive/straw brief, becomes the dominant read at 2-3× the coverage. Flag it alongside the density result rather than silently retinting.
- Read placement-struct field names from the C++ header (`UPROPERTY` lines) before probing palettes from Python, rather than guessing accessors. A `FSoftObjectPath` field has no asset-name getter in Python: take the name from `export_text().split('.')[-1]`.
- Placement loops feed a shared candidate array whose index seeds each PCG point. Reordering loops (for example outcrops before floor so the floor sees their rocks and cleared trees) changes every later point's seed and the tree-proximity inputs. Before reordering, list every graph using the node and its palettes (asset registry `PCGGraph` assets → nodes with that settings class); state the affected graphs in the evidence.

## Ground colour via the existing Landscape material

- Before swapping a surface texture or adding a ground ingredient, grep project evidence and the spec for prior trials of that texture, asset ID or role (including "do not restore" lines); reuse their verdict and change scope or mechanism rather than repeating a rejected variant.
- When a scanned litter surface reads as flat gravel, fix readability before depth:
  - Steepen its contrast about a fixed pivot (`.12*pow(L/.12, 1.35)`) so single leaves separate.
  - Multiply in large-scale variation from a two-octave value noise of world XY at ~3–11 m: damp hollows ×(.58,.52,.47) and warmer dry drifts ×(1.22,1.08,.90). The noise lives as a local-struct method placed before any local variable that shadows an intrinsic such as `floor`; better, name the variable `litter` so later `floor()` calls still work.
  - Hash with a sin-free hash (Hoskins: `p=frac(i.xyx*.1031); p+=dot(p,p.yzx+33.33); frac((p.x+p.y)*p.z)`), reusing one the material already has. `frac(sin(dot(p,k))*43758.5)` loses precision at world coordinates of 10⁵–10⁶ cm and varies by GPU.
  - Edits made directly in a Custom node have no regenerating script: say so in the evidence, and record the replaced line.
  - This works at every viewing angle and adds no texture samples.
- Before offering parallax occlusion mapping or Nanite displacement for a floor:
  - Confirm the surface has a height/displacement texture; imported Megascans Medium tier carries only colour, normal and ORM (the High tier adds `_H`).
  - POM flattens or swims at grazing angles, which is most of the floor at player height. It adds 8–32 height samples per pixel to a material near the sampler limit, and it never changes silhouettes or receives shadows from clutter. Recommend Nanite displacement instead; the working recipe is in `ue-landscape-and-foliage` `references/landscape.md` → "Material displacement on a Nanite landscape".
  - Treat it as a later, approval-gated trial after contrast and variation; turning Nanite on for the landscape changes the saved map. Ship the first trial subtle (a few cm, softened height) and offer more depth on request: an over-strong first pass reads as choppy and costs the user a review round.
- Judge a floor-material change from a player-height view over bare floor as well as the user's camera: added grass or clutter hides the floor in the shared view, and a crop's brightness spread mixes in vegetation.
- When one texture input feeds road, field and forest terms of a Custom HLSL node, add a separate input and change only the forest term; mask an added normal map by the forest mask times (1 − road-paint layer). Adding a Custom input logs a compile error until it is connected; judge the final recompile, not that transient line. If the node is already wired, do not add the input to it (see the Custom-node rule in SKILL.md): wrap it with a new `ForestFloor`-style Custom node that takes `Prev` (the old output) plus the same mask/texture inputs and returns `Prev + (new_term - old_term) * forest_mask`, keeping the recomputed old term bit-for-bit identical to the original code. Script it (`build()` idempotent, finds nodes by `description`) and keep a copy of the new node's code on disk for A/B swaps.
- Prove a new floor node drives the intended area before judging colour: temporarily set its code to `return lerp(Prev, float3(1,0,0), saturate(Mask));`, capture, then restore the saved code and assert it matches the file. For a floor A/B in one session, swap the code to `return Prev;` (old look), recompile, wait ~25-30 s for shaders, shoot, and restore.
- Floor brightness is not a material problem under auto exposure: darkening the floor albedo 30% changed nothing visible because eye adaptation rebalances the frame. Before changing albedo for brightness, check what the bright region actually is (in road-eye views the lower frame is mostly road and shoulders, not forest floor) and measure full-frame luma plus clipped-pixel share against the reference frames. UE5's default auto exposure bias is 1.0, not 0: an unbound post-process volume with `override_auto_exposure_bias` at 0 is -1 EV. Treat exposure as a scene-wide lighting decision for the creative owner, and check shaded views and the high overview too, since they darken with it.

## Verification set

Run after Generate, in this order, and record results in the evidence doc:

1. Height delta vs a saved pre-change height dump (line traces ignoring the forest actor on a 4 m grid): min/max/mean, slope median/p95/max, collar cells exactly 0. Roadbed: for each sample, trace the Landscape's `SplineMeshComponent`s directly; samples that hit a road must change by exactly 0, and the road must sit a few cm above the heightfield collision (none buried). For road clearance of generated items, use the same direct traces against the visible, colliding road spline meshes (bounds from `SystemLibrary.get_component_bounds`, skip invisible/collisionless ones), not road paint weights: a road need not be painted on the Landscape, and a paint-weight check then passes vacuously. Prove any clearance probe can detect positives first (a 2 m grid scan of the box must hit road cells) before trusting zero hits; check fallen trunks at several points along their length, not the pivot. Render a before/after hillshade from the two dumps (3x vertical exaggeration) as the overview evidence; obliques are usually blocked by trees.
2. Instance counts by mesh, site count, and snow/cover metrics through the project's existing checker. After removing meshes from a palette, run `PCGComponent.cleanup(True)` and list ISM components still on the actor: survivors are orphans PCG no longer tracks (neither Generate nor Cleanup removes them, and they save into the map). Destroy them with `component.destroy_component(component)`, then Generate.
3. Repeat Generate: sorted per-instance transform digest and height hash identical.
4. Save task-owned packages, then recount the actor's total instances against the Generate digest (orphans have appeared after Generate). Relaunch and compare graph settings, heights and digest before regenerating (cold reload).
5. Frame cost: single `PerformanceService.frame_timing()` readings swing by tens of percent. Preferred: a Slate post-tick callback that pins the camera every tick, applies one configuration (console variables such as `r.Nanite.Tessellation`/`landscape.RenderNanite`, or `set_visibility` on the new ISM components), waits ~8 s, then records the median `gpu_ms` of 90 frames, cycles through all configurations and ends by repeating the first one; the repeat matched to 0.01 ms, so differences of ~0.05 ms are real. Restore every toggle at the end and write results to a Saved/ JSON. Make the toggled mesh set a parameter of that helper file (name substrings across ISM and `InstancedSkinnedMeshComponent`s) and edit the file; string-replacing its source at runtime breaks on CRLF line endings and burns retries. A ready helper that records render-thread and GPU medians per hidden category or per ISM property override is `unreal-asset-sourcing` `scripts/category_cost_ab.py`. Otherwise take at least two readings per state in separate calls, toggling the new components' visibility for the comparison (`set_visibility(False, False)` per ISM component, confirmed with a capture, then restored and checked against the digest), and report ranges with the conditions (Editor viewport vs PIE). Compare only readings taken at the same camera: view content dominates the cost, so an earlier pass's numbers from another viewpoint are not a baseline. Discard readings of hundreds of ms or more: check `Saved/VibeUE/Signals/editor-<pid>-health.json` `gameThreadStallSeconds`, which shows an Editor stall, not render cost. Run the orphan check first, or the readings include leftover instances. For a PIE reading at the Editor comparison camera: start PIE with `LevelEditorSubsystem.editor_request_begin_play()`, wait about 30 s, and move the PIE `DefaultPawn` to the camera location minus its `base_eye_height` (0 here) with `set_actor_location`. Then set `PlayerController.set_control_rotation` to the camera rotation, read back `PlayerCameraManager` location and rotation, and wait about 25 s before measuring. Capture `source='game'` and then the Editor view at the same camera to confirm both render the same content. End PIE with `editor_request_end_play()` and confirm no dirty packages. If `frame_timing()` reports 0 ms render thread, say the metric is unavailable rather than comparing it with older render-thread figures. For motion cost, fly the editor camera along a fixed path with a slate post-tick callback and compare median/p95 frame time; when several features changed together, isolate the suspect by toggling one feature for a second run before attributing the cost.
6. Player-height and oblique captures at a known feature site; compare against the reference photos, not just the previous capture. After an Editor launch, call `PerformanceService.set_background_throttling(False)` and wait for streaming before any capture: an unfocused Editor runs ~1 FPS, Nanite foliage stays unstreamed and trees render as coarse blocks or flat cards that look like a content regression. Do not resize the Nanite streaming pool to test it; that flushes everything. Place the camera with a ground trace that ignores the forest actor, since traces from above hit tree crowns and give an aerial view. When the user reports a problem from a screenshot, read their current viewport with `UnrealEditorSubsystem.get_level_viewport_camera_info()` before any change and reuse that exact location/rotation for the labelled before/after. A computed close-up can land inside a tree's low branches and mislead; aim at a specific instance's transform and check the frame before using it. Choose new review views by capture, not by a trace-based sightline search: list 6 spread standing spots with yaws, capture them in one slate post-tick pass, judge the contact sheet by eye, and move blocked ones a few metres. A single horizontal complex trace through the generated forest can block the game thread indefinitely, and a time budget checked between traces cannot interrupt it.
   - In that capture pass, re-apply `set_level_viewport_camera_info` on every tick until the shot file exists: the Editor camera can keep drifting on its own (stuck navigation input), and frames then come out black or uniform grey from kilometres away underground. Check `get_level_viewport_camera_info()` against the set position whenever a capture is black.
   - `take_high_res_screenshot` requests made while shaders compile (e.g. right after a material recompile) can be dropped silently: delete the target file first, and re-request if it has not appeared ~15 s later instead of advancing.
   - Compare A/Bs as full-resolution crops of the same region side by side; a downscaled contact sheet hides cm-scale ground changes.
